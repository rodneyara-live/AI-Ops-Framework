# Chief of Staff

## Role purpose

The Chief of Staff is the CEO's force multiplier. Their function is not to manage a function; it is to ensure the CEO can operate at maximum effectiveness: filter, prioritize, anticipate, and close the cycles the CEO cannot close alone without delegating the decision. At FinCo, where the CEO is also Chief AI Officer and Acting Product Lead, the CoS is the one who turns vision into operating system, and who responds when the system fails.

This role does not report *to* the company; it reports *to the CEO*. Its success is measured by the CEO's ability to focus on what matters, the quality of the operating system it maintains, and the absence of open cycles without an owner.

---

## Responsibilities

**CEO extension**
- Act as the CEO's proxy in operational meetings, reviews, and coordination where their presence is not needed but their judgment is
- Prepare the CEO for all high-value meetings: pre-briefs, context material, pending decisions
- Manage the CEO's strategic agenda: what comes in, what is delegated, what is postponed, what disappears
- Translate the CEO's decisions and priorities into structured action for the team

**Operating system**
- Design, maintain, and evolve the FinCo-OS vault as the company's operational source of truth
- Ensure the canonical record is complete, up-to-date, and reliable for the team, agents, and CEO
- Define document hierarchy and QMS governance standards
- Ensure every work session with operational impact generates structured evidence in the vault

**DRI accountability**
- Maintain the DRI Map as a control board for critical operation components
- Ensure every component has an assigned owner, defined next action, and evidence of progress
- Detect blockers, escalate them to the CEO when they exceed the autonomous resolution threshold, and close the loop
- Lead the weekly Check/Act/Plan cycle as facilitator and as DRI of the process itself

**Strategic initiatives**
- Take ownership of cross-cutting projects that have no natural owner in the current structure
- Coordinate across functions (ops, product, development, compliance) when work crosses domains without a clear DRI
- Ensure formal decisions are documented, communicated, and executed, without exception

**Knowledge and internal communication**
- Convert conversations, meetings, and decisions into structured, agent-readable knowledge
- Process meeting minutes, extract decisions and commitments, and distribute them in the vault
- Maintain the internal communication cadence: the team knows what was decided, who decided it, and why

**Team operations**
- Design and maintain the team's operating cadence (Team Sync, Check/Act/Plan, agentic standups)
- Ensure management tools (Linear, Slack, Atlas) are configured and used consistently
- Coordinate onboarding of new team members into the operating system

**Operational compliance**
- Maintain visibility over the status of compliance controls (applicable local debt-collection regulations and applicable regulations)
- Ensure operational processes are designed from compliance-first, not as a later layer

---

## Role competencies

**Strategic**
- Ability to turn vision into structure: takes an ambiguous direction and converts it into process, owner, and evidence
- Systemic thinking: understands how pieces affect each other before moving any of them
- Judgment to distinguish urgent from important, and manage both without sacrificing the system
- Discretion: the CoS operates with full access to the CEO's context; that requires judgment about what is shared, when, and how

**Operational**
- Design and maintenance of management systems (QMS, PDCA, document control)
- Cycle management: opens, executes, closes, and generates evidence; no indefinitely open cycles
- High-quality written communication: the role generates documentation that others use, so precision and clarity are not optional
- Effective facilitation: leads meetings toward decisions, not conversation

**Technological**
- Natural flow with the operational stack tools: Obsidian/vault, GitHub, Linear, Slack, Atlas
- Sufficient understanding of the technical stack to make informed operational decisions without relying on constant translation
- Ability to work with AI agents as part of the daily operational flow, not as an occasional tool

## Scope of authority

| Decision | Authority |
|---|---|
| Vault structure and governance documents | Autonomous |
| DRI assignments and next actions on the DRI Map | Autonomous |
| Meeting cadence and format | Autonomous |
| CEO agenda and priorities | Coordinated with CEO |
| Contractor onboarding | Coordinated with CEO |
| Product decisions with roadmap impact | Gate: CEO |
| External commitments (contracts, clients) | Gate: CEO |
